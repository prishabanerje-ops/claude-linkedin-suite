The one-line diagnosis

Your content isn't the problem — distribution is, and it's shrinking.

People who see your posts engage at an above-average rate. Too few people see them. The sample post reached ~3% of your followers against a healthy band of 8–12%, and your whole account is down 24% week-over-week. Every fix below targets reach volume, not writing quality.

Ranked gaps & fixes

Ordered by expected impact on reach. Severity reflects how much each is likely holding you back.

01

Format: text-only is the weakest format for reach

CriticalBenchmark

On personal profiles, 2026 reach multipliers rank: polls 1.64× › documents 1.45× › images 1.18× › video 1.10× › text-only 0.88× (below baseline). Your post was pure text. Source: van der Blom, Algorithm Insights (personal-profile reach multipliers).

FixMove your best insights into native document carousels (~9 slides, short copy) — the confirmed sweet spot. Keep an occasional poll for spike reach. When you do post text, lead with a hook + one native image.
02

Early comments — not likes — break the test batch

CriticalBenchmark

LinkedIn shows a post to a slice of your network first, then expands only if signals are strong. The weighting is comments & saves ≫ reactions. Your post earned just 1 comment. Source: Sprout, Hootsuite, van der Blom (3-0 verified). The fixed "golden hour" timer was refuted — it's continuous test-then-expand.

FixWithin the first 60–90 min, reply fast and seed 2–3 genuine comments (you already did this with Somya — keep it up). End every post with one sharp question, not a generic "thoughts?".
03

Dwell time is the #1 confirmed ranking signal

HighBenchmark

Plain text gets a 2-second scan; carousels and video hold attention far longer. Dwell time is the only mechanism LinkedIn officially confirms in its own engineering research. Source: LinkedIn Engineering — feed dwell-time ranking (first-party).

FixThis is why format matters so much — swipeable documents rack up dwell. Use a strong hook, generous line breaks, and put the payoff mid-post so people stop and read instead of scrolling past.
04

Inconsistent cadence + a declining trend

HighMeasured

Your impressions are down 24% WoW, and LinkedIn itself is nudging you to "post weekly for 4× profile views." Distribution trust erodes without a steady rhythm. Note: the specific "3–5 posts/week sweet spot" stat was refuted in verification — so this is about consistency, not a magic frequency.

FixPost 2–4× per week, consistently, on the same core topics (SEO / GEO / AI-search) so the algorithm learns who to show you to. Rhythm beats volume.
05

Audience is fragmented (minor drag)

LowMeasured

Your top job title is only 4% of followers, and ~17% are core SEO/marketing. The random test-batch therefore includes non-SEO people who won't engage with SEO posts — a small early-signal drag. This is NOT a quality problem — your audience is relevant and senior. See the audience panel below.

FixKeep doing what's working: steady growth (+1%/wk) and commenting in SEO/GEO circles to concentrate your following. No drastic action needed.
04

Keep vs. ignore

✓ Already doing right

  • ✓No outbound link in the post body. Link posts are the lowest-reach format (~3.25–3.7%). You avoided the single biggest reach-killer — put sources in the first comment.
  • ✓Strong, specific hook + above-average engagement rate. The writing genuinely isn't the problem.
  • ✓Quality audience: 42% Senior, ~17% core SEO/marketing, steady +1%/week growth.
  • ✓Fast replies to commenters — exactly the behavior that expands reach.

✕ Don't waste effort on

  • ✕Hashtag tuning. No reliable 2026 reach effect survived verification — 3 vs 5 hashtags won't move it.
  • ✕Chasing a "golden hour" timer. Refuted — distribution is continuous test-then-expand, not a 60-minute gate.
  • ✕Obsessing over exact time-of-day. No reliable 2026 optimum established; consistency beats clock-watching.
  • ✕Buying reach with volume. Posting more often without better format/dwell just spreads the same weak signal thinner.

The play

Your next 7 days

A prioritized sequence — top items move reach the most. Run it for two weeks and re-check the 7-day impressions trend.

1
Ship one document carousel
Turn your strongest SEO/GEO insight (e.g. the "citation share" idea) into a ~9-slide native carousel. Highest-reach format available to you (1.45×) and it maximizes dwell time.
2
Engineer the first 90 minutes
Post when you can be present. Reply to every comment fast, seed 2–3 real ones, and close the post with a single pointed question. This is what triggers expansion beyond the test batch.
3
Keep links out of the body
If you're citing a source or your own article, drop it in the first comment — never the post body.
4
Lock a 2–4×/week rhythm
Same core topics, steady cadence, to rebuild the 24% you've lost and teach the algorithm who to show you to.
5
Concentrate the audience daily
Comment substantively on 3–5 SEO/GEO peers' posts a day. Compounds your ~17% core and sharpens every future test batch.

Skip entirely:  hashtag counts · best-time-of-day tuning · posting more without better format. None of these survived verification as reach levers.
